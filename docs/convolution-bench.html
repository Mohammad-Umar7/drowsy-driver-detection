A kernel is not a mysterious thing. It is a handful of numbers you slide across a picture, multiplying and adding. Step through it below and watch the arithmetic happen.

1 · Start with a single row

Six numbers. Think of them as one thin strip of a photo — 9 is bright, 1 is dark. You can see the edge instantly. The computer cannot.

Flat stretches give 0. The one place brightness changes gives 8. Those two numbers [+1, −1] just became an edge detector — that is all a kernel is.

2 · A real picture is 2-D, so the kernel is a square

A row only lets you compare left↔right. To also compare up↔down you need a square: 3 rows × 3 columns = 9 numbers. Identical idea, one extra direction.

The open eye produces edges scattered around the iris and both lids. The closed eye produces one thick unbroken band. A later layer only has to notice “one band, not scattered rings” to call it shut.

4 · Nobody chooses the 9 numbers

Every kernel above was written by hand so you could see what it does. In the real network they start as random junk — press Random (untrained) above and look at the mush it produces. That is exactly what our model output before training.

Training then nudges those 9 numbers, thousands of times, in whatever direction reduces the error. They drift on their own into edge detectors, curve detectors, blob detectors — because those happen to be useful. Nobody programmed “look for an eyelid.”

The numbers in your actual model

layer 1   32 kernels × 9 numbers  =  288 knobs   →  edges, gradients
layer 2   64 kernels  →  corners, curves, blobs
layer 3   128 kernels  →  eyelid shapes, iris-like rings
output    2 numbers  →  open / closed

total 139,426 knobs  ·  trained in 7.2 min  ·  98.48% on faces it had never seen

Compare: connecting all 1,024 pixels straight to 1,000 neurons would need 1,024,000 knobs in a single layer — and it still would not find an edge that moved two pixels sideways. Sliding 9 numbers does both jobs.

convolution  =  slide a small set of numbers over a big set of numbers, multiplying and adding.
CNN  =  a network built out of those sliding rules, stacked in layers.
